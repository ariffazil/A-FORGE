## Methodology

**Scoring anchors:**
- 10 = perfect, would not change
- 7 = good, minor quibbles

## Pattern Analysis

The functional/action scheme has one structural weakness: **pattern inconsistency**.

| Pattern | Skills | Count |
|---------|--------|-------|
| noun-verb | self-audit, health-sweep | 2 |
| noun-noun | skill-map, skill-forge, tool-gate | 3 |
| verb-verb | entropy-reduce | 1 |
| single noun | watchdog | 1 |

Three different patterns across 7 names. The existing Layer 2 names (self_check, identity_bind, evidence_tag, verdict_grammar) are all noun-verb. Only self-audit and health-sweep follow that convention.

This isn't fatal — the names are still functional — but it means the naming layer doesn't feel cohesive. A new agent loading these skills won't see a clear pattern.

---

## Verdict: USE_WITH_RESERVATIONS

**Reasoning:**

The functional scheme scores well on clarity (8.3 avg) and cognitive fit (8.6 avg). These names tell you what the skill DOES, which is the primary job of a Layer 1 operational name. The strongest name — self-audit (9.2) — is genuinely excellent.

The reservations are structural:

1. **entropy-reduce** is the weakest link (7.2). The verb-verb pattern is grammatically awkward and breaks consistency. Consider: `entropy-sweep` or `chaos-reduce` or `order-restore`.

2. **watchdog** breaks pattern as a single noun. Consider: `system-watchdog` or `federation-watchdog` to maintain the compound pattern.

3. **Pattern inconsistency** (3 different patterns across 7 names) means this doesn't read as a "naming system" — it reads as 7 individually reasonable names that happen to coexist.

**Recommendation:** Use as-is for 5 of 7. Rework entropy-reduce and watchdog to match the noun-verb or noun-noun compound pattern. Then re-evaluate consistency.
